A Good Life.

One step at a time.

Values → Goals → Execution

Values inform goals. Goals guide execution. Execution is how you pursue goals while remaining aligned with your values.

Values

What do I value?

Values are the things that matter to you and that you want your life to reflect. They are the foundation. Everything else follows from them.

  • Health
  • Freedom
  • Friendship
  • Creativity
  • Adventure
  • Knowledge
  • Love
  • Beauty
  • Community
Goals

What do I want my life to become?

Goals are the high-level directions for the life you want to build. They are shaped by your values and give purpose to daily decisions.

  • Become physically fit
  • Build a rich social life
  • Become financially independent
  • Become an excellent filmmaker
  • Live somewhere I love
  • Develop deep friendships
  • Become well-read
  • Travel extensively
Execution

How do I pursue it?

Execution is the practical part of the framework. It asks three questions:

What should I add?
What should I maintain?
What should I remove?

Under each of these, consider both the things you have and the activities you do.

Add
Things
  • Running shoes
  • A camera
  • Books
  • A bicycle
Activities
  • Join a run club
  • Take a filmmaking class
  • Start reading
  • Plan a trip
Maintain
Activities
  • Regular exercise
  • Reading
  • Making films
  • Time with people you care about
Remove
Things
  • Possessions you don’t use
  • Things that create clutter or unnecessary expense
Activities
  • Excessive screen time
  • Sedentary habits
  • Activities that consume time without adding value

Things and activities influence one another. A camera enables filmmaking. Running shoes enable running. Changing what you have often changes what you do.

Running shoes → Running   ·   Camera → Filmmaking   ·   Bicycle → Cycling

Relationships are not treated as objects to add or remove. They tend to emerge from the life you build—from the activities you maintain and the places you put yourself.

Change what you have. Change what you do.
